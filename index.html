From wave optics to ray optics
A double-slit experiment viewed in angle. Make the wavelength shorter and watch the wave structure collapse toward the geometrical ray direction.
Wave optics visible
Two complementary pictures: in geometrical optics, light passing through the two slits continues as two straight rays/beams. In wave optics, each finite slit diffracts; the two diffracted fields overlap on the screen and interfere. The graph below shows the far-field angular intensity, where the two contributions share essentially the same forward direction as the wavelength becomes very small.

What the graph shows

The coloured curve is the Fraunhofer double-slit intensity. In the geometrical-optics picture there are two straight transmitted rays/beams, one through each slit. In the far-field angular plot they correspond to the same forward angle, θ = 0.

I(θ) ∝ sinc²(πa sinθ/λ)
× cos²(πd sinθ/λ)

Ray optics is not different physics. It is a useful approximation when the wavelength-dependent structure is too small to matter at the scale or resolution of the experiment.

Two shrinking angular scales

Diffraction shrinks roughly as λ/a, while the double-slit fringe spacing shrinks roughly as λ/d. With the geometry fixed, both approach zero as λ → 0.

Why this becomes “ray optics”

If those angular features are much smaller than the resolution you care about, resolving the full wave pattern adds little useful information. The field then appears concentrated around the geometrical ray direction.
